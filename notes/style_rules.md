# Style rules applied to the article

The article is written as a story for a general reader, in the plain-writing style. These rules replace the earlier report-style rules, which asked for "reversal network" instead of "trap" and for headings that start with a subject and a verb.

1. Story order. Hook (one question, two answers), premise (Kahneman's two systems), short setup, findings grouped by when each mode is enough, then guidance.
2. Plain words for readers who have never seen the test.
   - "trap" is the network where the first number points the wrong way. "control" is the matching network where it does not.
   - Describe a network by what happened to it, never by its ID, e.g., "the volume question", not "NW-6731".
   - Flash and Standard are the two mode names. "Standard" means standard Agent mode.
3. Two scores, named where they are used. "Met every check" means all four criteria. "Reached the right conclusion" means the final claim was right.
4. Say which round a number comes from at first use.
5. Plain writing. No dashes, colons only before lists, no filler, no bold as decoration, sentence-case headings, straight quotes. Use "e.g." for a single example.
